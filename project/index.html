01 / Colour

The primary never carries status

Three blues were tested. Kansanshi was chosen because it holds as an 11px label, a 6px progress fill and a 1px focus ring without ever reading as a state of its own. Ledger sits too near ink, so a focus ring stops registering on a sunlit screen. Signal is bright enough that a blue progress fill starts reading as a status.

Separability test

Primary, info and the three semantics, in every form they appear in: as rendered, washed out as on a sunlit tablet, and stripped of colour entirely.

The brand device

Marketing uses a red short rule, a gold long rule and heavy condensed caps. Red there competes with withheld, and gold at that weight competes with warning. The device survives into the product; the red does not. The short rule becomes ink, gold darkens to #B08A2E, and it is permitted in exactly one place: a 2px rule under a page title. Gold never appears as a fill, a dot, text or a background, and warning never appears as a rule. The two cannot be mistaken because they never share a shape.

04 / The hardest colour problem

Rigging period band

Period colours are physical tape on a sling, so they cannot be recoloured to suit the palette. They are kept apart from status by shape and containment instead. A period colour is always a 16px square paired with a mono period name, and only ever appears inside a band or a cell labelled as a period. Status is always a circle plus a sentence-case word, and never a square. A red square means the item passed inspection in the red quarter. A red dot means the item is withheld. The two never appear in the same column.

05 / Voice

The page subtitle is a thesis statement

Opinionated, never hedged. Numbers before adjectives. Name the owner and the cause. Show the working. No exclamation marks, no emoji, no em dashes.
